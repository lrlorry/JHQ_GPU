Where JHQ Actually Wins

The fronts below were measured before two things came to light: the OpenAI3 rows used a primary codebook the paper's construction cannot build at that M, and the search that produced every row departs from Algorithm 1 in ways that had not been priced. What each departure costs is now measured. The crossover those fronts show does not survive.

Retracted — the dimension crossover

The 1.42× at 1536d and 2.17× at 3072d were taken at M=96, where Equation 4 needs Ds = d/M to divide B=8. That admits Ds ∈ {1,2,4,8}, so M ∈ {d, d/2, d/4, d/8} — 192 is the smallest legal value at 1536d and 384 at 3072d. It is not simply M ≥ d/8: at 768d, M=128 gives Ds=6, above d/8 and still inadmissible. At those M the construction runs, and CAGRA is ahead on both sets: 0.9850 at 11,227 QPS against 0.9856 at 20,559 on OpenAI3-1536, 0.9847 at 5,635 against 0.9849 at 10,354 on OpenAI3-3072. The primary code cannot be made shorter than d/8 bytes under that construction, which is why the front moves.

What survives is on this page below, unchanged: the scale results, the capacity result, and the compressed-graph ceiling. None of them depend on the code that was re-measured.

What the extension changed

weakened

The dimension crossover no longer describes the whole high-recall range

The two claimed points survive almost intact — 1.42× → 1.37× at 1536d, 2.17× → 2.09× at 3072d. But CAGRA now reaches 0.997 on both sets and takes the frontier above 0.992, which the truncated grid hid.

reversed

On 768 dimensions JHQ holds no frontier point at all

Vogue and arXiv are CAGRA's from 0.96 to 1.0000. The earlier tables showed CAGRA stopping at 0.990 — that ceiling was the sweep's, not the method's.

strengthened

Compressed graph search saturates, and scale is where JHQ is alone

int8 CAGRA tops out at 0.9455–0.9780 even with four times the search width. On the two largest sets JHQ owns everything above that ceiling, up to 0.9936.

What the paper can and cannot say

×  not supported
  • “JHQ overtakes CAGRA at high recall.” Measured at an M where the paper's codebook builds, CAGRA leads on both OpenAI3 sets outright — 20,559 QPS against 11,227 at 1536d, 10,354 against 5,635 at 3072d — and holds every frontier point above 0.975 on the 768d sets.
  • “Only JHQ runs at this scale.” int8 CAGRA builds 17.8M vectors in 19.5 GiB and reaches 0.9780 at 386,824 QPS — 12.9× JHQ's throughput below that recall.
  • “The optimized search is JHQ, just on a GPU.” Both departures are now priced. The prefix cascade at 1/4 costs 0.9747 → 0.9647; at 1/2 it buys 38% for a change inside the build's own noise. Per-thread retention at depth 4 differs from depth 8 in 1 query's result set per 1000, and depth 4 is the only value that fits in registers at BLOCK=1024.
  • “123–219× faster to build.” The CPU reference ran at nlist=256 against GPU runs at 1024–16384, and re-measuring it today gives 1.5–2.7× slower numbers than those on record.
✓  supported by these runs
  • Above the compressed-graph ceiling, JHQ is the only method that runs. On BGE-M3 that band starts at 0.938, on Stella-TREC24 at 0.978, and JHQ reaches 0.9881 and 0.9936 respectively.
  • The ceiling is the representation, not the search. Stella int8 moves 0.9762 → 0.9771 → 0.9780 as itopk goes 128 → 256 → 512 while throughput falls fourfold; on arXiv it goes backwards.
  • fp32 graph search does not fit. Measured VRAM matches N·d·b + N·deg·4 within 0.1% across every dataset, so BGE-M3 needs 39.70 GiB against a 31.36 GiB card.
  • In the 0.98–0.99 band at high dimension, JHQ leads on throughput — 1.37× at 1536d and 2.09× at 3072d, with a smaller resident index.

Two harness faults found on the way

The scalar quantiser ran once per search configuration

scalar.train and scalar.transform sat inside the grid loop, so every itopk pushed the whole dataset through quantisation again — 73 GiB per configuration at 17.8M×1024. That is why the first Stella int8 attempt hit a 90-minute limit without writing a row. Hoisted out, the same measurement takes 8 minutes.

The recorded reason for the BGE-M3 out-of-memory was wrong

The note claimed the index behind it would have been under 4 GiB, counting only the adjacency. CAGRA keeps the dataset on the device for search, and the measured VRAM says so to within 0.1%. The capacity result stands — 39.70 GiB against 31.36 — but it is the index not fitting, not an avoidable staging copy.
