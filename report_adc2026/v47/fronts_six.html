Six fronts, re-measured: 1.3× to 2.6× faster at the same recall

Every gain below leaves the JHQ algorithm untouched. Two came from bugs — a selection buffer sorted on the wrong predicate, and a sigma that silently reached zero above 2.1M training rows — and the rest from two parameters nobody had swept: nlist, whose optimum moves along the front, and the coarse quantizer's training set, which matters below about twenty points per centroid and costs above it.

Where the speedup comes from

Code: v45 sorts the selection buffer when it is full rather than when it passes the target (+11 to +38%), and v47 factorises the primary LUT along equation 4's Cartesian product, 32 entries a subspace instead of 256 (+6 to +14.5%). Parameters: an nlist whose optimum moves along the front, so every front here is an envelope over four values, and a training set that is worth +42 to +96% on the two largest datasets and −47% on Vogue.

What it cost to build

Stella is the largest case: 17.8M vectors, thirteen times the training set and twice the lists. The index goes from 6.0 s to 11.1 s to build, and VRAM from 20,547 to 20,837 MiB. add() dominates the build and does not change; k-means is a GEMM this card finishes in seconds.

What these numbers are not

Recall carries a 1e-3 floor across builds — training is not reproducible, and three cold-cache runs of one binary returned 0.9852 / 0.9842 / 0.9849 on Vogue with identical candidate counts. At the top the front is near-vertical, so that floor is worth 2–3× of apparent QPS and interpolation there is meaningless; the report compares that band at matched nlist and nprobe instead. One alpha throughout.
