JHQ on GPU · measured 8 September 2026 · RTX 5090

Two bugs and a parameter

JHQ got 1.3× to 2.3× faster at matched recall on all six datasets, with the algorithm untouched. Almost none of it came from the kernel arithmetic the optimisation effort had been aimed at. Two of the three sources were defects, and the largest was a parameter that had never been sized.

The frontier did not change hands. Where fp32 CAGRA can build an index, it still owns high recall. What JHQ owns, it owns because nothing else runs there — and that was already true before this work. The speedup makes the alone-territory cheaper, not larger.

What moved

v45
Both selection buffers sorted the moment the count passed the target. cap is the power of two at or above target + BLOCK, so after each compaction the count sat at the target and the next chunk pushed it past again — a full 2048-element bitonic sort per chunk to absorb a handful of survivors. The bound the buffer needs is cnt + BLOCK ≤ cap.
+11 to +38%
v46
cublasSdot(n * d_, …) passes an int. At d=1024 the product wraps negative above 2,097,152 rows; BLAS returns without writing the result for a non-positive length, so sigma came out zero and equation 4 placed every primary centroid at the origin. No error anywhere. It read as an nlist ceiling for two runs, because nlist and the training set had always been raised together.
fixes 0.48 → 0.99
v47
Equation 4 builds each subspace's codewords as a Cartesian product, so T[c] = Thi[c>>4] + Tlo[c&15] exactly. 32 table entries a subspace instead of 256: 8× smaller, 16× cheaper to build, and conflict-free in shared memory where the 256-entry table took up to an 8-way conflict.
+6 to +14.5%
config
The coarse quantizer had been trained on JHQ_N_TRAIN=100000 at every nlist — 6.1 points per centroid on Stella, against the 39 FAISS treats as a floor. Better centroids partition more evenly, so a fixed nprobe touches fewer vectors: measured candidates at nprobe=128 fall from 226,410 to 146,535 at higher recall.
+5 to +96%
v48
Squared-distance table entries are non-negative, so a partial sum past the ck-th best cannot reach the top-ck and the remaining subspaces need be neither gathered nor looked up. Exact. The exit has to be per warp — lanes walk subspaces in lockstep — and the maximum over 32 lanes is far above the mean.
−1 to −3%

v43 and v44 carry no speed of their own. v43 added the two diagnostics the rest of this rests on; v44 replaced a probe selector that held all nlist scores in one block's shared memory and could not launch above nlist=16384.

What it costs to build

The objection this project raised against itself was that throughput bought with a bigger index is not free: nlist doubled and the training set grew thirteen-fold. Measured on cold caches, it very nearly is free.

Limits of these numbers

  • The new fronts are incomplete at the top. This sweep took four nprobe values — 8, 32, 128, 256 — at one alpha. It reaches lower recall than the published fronts and does not reach as high: Vogue tops out at 0.9827 against the published 0.9954. Every comparison above is on the overlap.
  • Recall carries a 1e-3 floor across builds. Training is not reproducible: three cold-cache runs of one binary returned 0.9852 / 0.9842 / 0.9849 on Vogue with identical candidate counts, so coarse assignment is deterministic and the codebooks differ in their last bits. Any recall difference below 1e-3 between two builds means nothing — which is the floor v47's 5e-4 agreement and all of v48's recall movement sit under.
  • The baselines are older measurements. cuVS fronts come from commit 305e763 and were not re-run. Only the JHQ rows are new.
  • One alpha, one Br. alpha=100 and Br=8 throughout. The ranking loss saturates at alpha=20 on Stella and 50 on Vogue, so a per-dataset alpha is still unmeasured on the other four.
  • The published fronts may carry the sigma bug. The threshold is 231/d rows: 699,050 on OpenAI3-3072 and 1,398,101 on OpenAI3-1536, far below Stella's 2,097,152. Any historical run at a large training set on those two is suspect and has not been audited.

What the diagnostics say to do next

v43 measured where recall is lost. Across three datasets and a 32× range of nprobe, everything downstream of coarse routing — the equation-4 primary code, the ADC scan, the top-ck selection, the residual refinement — together loses between 0.0013 and 0.0051 of the neighbours routing hands it. At nprobe=8, 99.3% of Vogue's shortfall is a list that was never opened.

No work on the scan can raise recall. The compaction, the lookup, the table precision and the early-exit bound are all speed items with a half-point recall budget. Recall belongs to the coarse quantizer — which is also where the largest speedup came from, and it is the least optimised part of the system.

It also says the quantisation is not the weak link: a 1 bit/dim primary code plus a Br-bit residual reproduces the exact ranking of what it is given to within half a point. That is a result about JHQ, and it is invisible in the recall number because routing dominates it.
