JHQ on GPU · 8 September 2026 · RTX 5090 · 536 measured points

Bytes, bugs and a parameter

At the same bytes per vector, JHQ reaches a recall ceiling 1.8 to 5.5 points above int8 CAGRA on all six datasets — and it is now 1.3× to 2.1× faster than the fronts this project published, with the algorithm untouched.

Almost none of the speedup came from the kernel arithmetic. Two of three sources were defects — a selection buffer sorted on the wrong predicate, and a sigma that reached zero without an error above 2.1M training rows — and the largest was a parameter nobody had sized: the coarse quantizer had been trained on about six points per centroid.

At equal bytes

JHQ stores a primary code of M bytes plus a residual of (d×Br+7)/8 — a byte per dimension at Br=8, not per subspace. That puts Br=8 within 1–8% of int8 CAGRA's footprint on every dataset here, which makes it the matched-memory operating point rather than an expensive one.

Where the speedup came from

v45
Both selection buffers sorted the moment the count passed the target. cap is the power of two at or above target + BLOCK, so after each compaction the count sat at the target and the next chunk pushed it past again — a full 2048-element bitonic sort per chunk to absorb a handful of survivors. The bound the buffer needs is cnt + BLOCK ≤ cap.
+11 to +38%
v46
cublasSdot(n * d_, …) takes an int. At d=1024 the product wraps negative above 2,097,152 rows; BLAS returns without writing the result for a non-positive length, so sigma came out zero and equation 4 placed every primary centroid at the origin. No error anywhere. It read as an nlist ceiling for two runs, because nlist and the training set had always been raised together.
fixes 0.48 → 0.99
v47
Equation 4 builds each subspace's codewords as a Cartesian product, so T[c] = Thi[c>>4] + Tlo[c&15] exactly. 32 table entries a subspace instead of 256: 8× smaller, 16× cheaper to build, and conflict-free in shared memory where the 256-entry table took up to an 8-way conflict. The identity is verified against the centroids at train time, not inferred.
+6 to +14.5%
config
The coarse quantizer had been trained on JHQ_N_TRAIN=100000 at every nlist — 6.1 points per centroid on Stella, against the 39 FAISS treats as a floor. Below about ten points per centroid k-means is degenerate — most centroids never move far from initialisation and the partition is wildly uneven — and repairing it cuts candidates at nprobe=128 from 226,410 to 146,535 at higher recall. The effect is monotone in points per centroid and crosses zero around twenty; see below.
+42 to +96% on the two largest;
−47% on Vogue
v48
Squared-distance entries are non-negative, so a partial sum past the ck-th best cannot reach the top-ck and the remaining subspaces need be neither gathered nor looked up. Exact. But the exit has to be per warp — lanes walk subspaces in lockstep — and the maximum over 32 lanes is far above the mean.
−1 to −3%

v43 and v44 carry no speed of their own. v43 added the diagnostics the rest rests on; v44 replaced a probe selector that held all nlist scores in one block's shared memory and could not launch above nlist=16384.

What it costs to build

The objection this project raised against itself was that throughput bought with a bigger index is not free: nlist doubled and the training set grew thirteen-fold. Measured on cold caches, it very nearly is.

Who owns which recall

JHQ does not beat a running baseline above R ≈ 0.88 on any dataset. Where fp32 CAGRA can build an index it owns high recall; JHQ's high-recall territory is BGE-M3 above 0.9428 and Stella above 0.9781, the two datasets where fp32 CAGRA needs more memory than the card has (67.8 GiB of 31.4 on Stella) and int8 CAGRA saturates below the range.

The 2× did not move the boundary; it made the alone-territory cheaper. What did change hands is below R = 0.88, where JHQ is now ahead of everything that runs — a band the published fronts never sampled, because their nprobe sweep started higher.

The candidate counts run both ways, and they are the whole effect. On Stella at 6.1 points a centroid, more training cuts candidates scanned from 226,410 to 146,535 — −35% at higher recall. On Vogue at 24.4, it raises them from 46,541 to 90,578 — +95%. Same knob, opposite directions, at matched recall.

Below roughly ten points a centroid, k-means is degenerate: most centroids never receive enough data to move far from where initialisation put them, so the partition is pathologically uneven and a query that opens nprobe lists opens some enormous ones. Repairing that is worth a great deal. Above about twenty, the quantizer is already reasonable and more training only makes it follow the density more sharply — more, smaller centroids where the data is dense, which is precisely where queries land. So it is not that a better-trained quantizer is faster; it is that an under-trained one is pathologically uneven, and the fix pays until the pathology is gone.

BGE-M3's nlist optimum stays unbracketed for a memory reason: 65,536 and 131,072 both fail in add() at the n × bpv residual buffer — 10.09M rows at 1,024 B is 10.3 GiB, and the extra centroid copies push past the 32 GiB card.

The top of the front cannot be interpolated

At high recall the front is nearly vertical, and recall varies between builds by up to 1e-3. In a vertical region that is worth 2–3× of apparent QPS, so interpolating one front against another there reads noise as a result — which produced a spurious "0.32× regression" on Vogue and "0.19×" on arXiv, both from comparing an endpoint against an interpolation.

The 4e-4 to 1.6e-3 recall deficit in every cell is equation 4, not a regression. The published fronts were built before build_analytical_cartesian existed, so their primary level is a Lloyd-refined product quantiser rather than equation 4's Cartesian product — measured at 0.027 better on its own, closed to about 0.004 once the 2000-iteration residual codebook runs. So this is the faithful codebook against a stronger non-paper one, and the faithful one is 1.10 to 1.26× faster for that difference.

What the diagnostics say to do next

v43 measured where recall is lost. Across three datasets and a 32× range of nprobe, everything downstream of coarse routing — the equation-4 primary code, the ADC scan, the top-ck selection, the residual refinement — together loses between 0.0013 and 0.0051 of the neighbours routing hands it. At nprobe=8, 99.3% of Vogue's shortfall is a list that was never opened.

No work on the scan can raise recall. The compaction, the lookup, the table precision and the early-exit bound are all speed items with a half-point recall budget. Recall belongs to the coarse quantizer — which is also where the largest speedup came from, and it is the least optimised part of the system.

It also says the quantisation is not the weak link: a 1 bit/dim primary code plus a Br-bit residual reproduces the exact ranking of what it is given to within half a point. That is a result about JHQ, and it is invisible in the recall number because routing dominates it.

Limits of these numbers

  • Recall carries a 1e-3 floor across builds. Training is not reproducible: three cold-cache runs of one binary returned 0.9852 / 0.9842 / 0.9849 on Vogue with identical candidate counts, so coarse assignment is deterministic and the codebooks differ in their last bits. Differences below 1e-3 between two builds mean nothing.
  • The baselines were not re-run. cuVS fronts come from commit 305e763 and a separate measurement session. Only the JHQ rows are new.
  • One alpha. alpha=100 throughout, matching the published sweep. The ranking loss saturates at alpha=20 on Stella and 50 on Vogue, so a per-dataset alpha is unmeasured on the other four.
  • The fronts plotted are not at the best nlist. They predate the sweep two sections up: on BGE-M3, Stella and OpenAI3-1536 a larger nlist is better, by up to 139% at Stella's top. The charts understate the method by that much, and BGE-M3's optimum is still unbracketed.
  • n_train and nlist are separated only on Stella. Elsewhere they moved together, — now separated on all six, which narrowed the claim rather than confirming it: see the section on points per centroid.
  • The sigma overflow did not reach the published results. Audited: the demo defaults to min(N, 100000) training rows and no recorded run set JHQ_N_TRAIN, so every historical row sits far below the 231/d threshold — 699,050 rows at d=3072, the tightest case. The bug was only reachable once the training set was raised.
