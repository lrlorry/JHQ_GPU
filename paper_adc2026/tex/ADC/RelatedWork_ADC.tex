\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}\label{sec:relatedwork}
\paragraph{Quantisation and grouped ADC.}
Product quantisation~\cite{pq} decomposes vectors into subspaces for compressed
storage and asymmetric distance computation. JHQ~\cite{jhq} supplies the
orthogonal transform, Cartesian primary codebook and residual hierarchy used
here. PQ Fast Scan~\cite{fastscan} and Quicker ADC~\cite{quickeradc} organise
small tables for efficient execution, and 16-entry component tables are
established practice there rather than something this paper introduces: our
$G{=}2$ is that same granularity. What we contribute is applying the
trade-off to JHQ's primary representation and measuring its interaction with
packed access and scan depth. Equation~\ref{eq:split}
is a direct consequence of that representation; arbitrary learned PQ
subcodebooks do not in general have the same internal separability.

\paragraph{Adaptive search and GPU systems.}
FAISS~\cite{faiss} provides sampled parameter autotuning, while learned early
termination~\cite{earlyterm} adapts search effort to a query. Our workload policy
uses an unlabelled self-agreement signal to choose JHQ's residual budget and
compares it with the exhaustive sweep it replaces. CAGRA~\cite{cagra} offers a
strong graph-based GPU baseline, and cuVS also implements IVF-PQ. The
evaluation compares against both and separates search throughput from
construction cost and resident memory.
