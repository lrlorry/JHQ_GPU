\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclu}
JHQ's Cartesian codebook makes its distance table exactly factorisable, and
the measurements say where that pays. $G{=}2$ has the highest throughput in 23
of 24 cells over six datasets, while $G{=}4$ and $G{=}8$ hold the same
footprint and differ only in lookups --- so table size alone does not
determine scan speed. Stage timings then explain why a refinement budget is
worth choosing at all: the scan this work makes cheap falls from $32\%$ of a
query to $22\%$ as dimension grows fourfold, and refinement rises from $57\%$
to $70\%$.

Calibrating that budget on sampled output agreement buys $1.05$--$2.77\times$
in ten of twelve held-out cells, and loses in the two where the sufficient
budget lies at or beyond the tested grid --- a condition visible from the
sweep before calibration is run. The evidence is one GPU, $k=10$ and
stationary query pools; drift, other $k$ and other devices are untested.
