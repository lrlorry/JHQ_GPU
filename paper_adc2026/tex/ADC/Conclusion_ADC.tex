\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclu}
JHQ's hierarchy creates two distinct GPU decisions. Its Cartesian primary
representation supports exact table factorisation, but useful granularity
depends on both table storage and the work needed to access it. Its residual
budget can be selected from sampled output stability, provided that calibration
cost and sampling risk are included in the decision. The held-out comparisons
show why neither a cheap constant nor self-agreement alone settles the quality
question. JHQ-GPU occupies a useful operating region alongside IVF-PQ
and CAGRA; the evidence is limited to one GPU, $k=10$, and stationary
query pools. Cross-device behaviour, other $k$ and drift remain future work.
