\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclu}
JHQ's Cartesian codebook makes its distance table exactly factorisable, and
the measurements show where that pays. $G{=}2$ has the highest throughput in
23 of 24 cells over six datasets, while the equal-footprint $G{=}4$ and
$G{=}8$ comparison shows that lookup work also matters. Refinement accounts
for $57\%$ and $70\%$ of measured work on Vogue and OpenAI-3072 at probe 32
and $\alpha=100$, motivating workload-specific budget selection.

Sampled output agreement yields mean selected-arm QPS of
$1.05$--$2.77\times$ the fixed-$\alpha$ arm in ten of twelve held-out cells.
On arxiv it selects larger budgets that improve mean recall but lower
throughput relative to fixed $\alpha=100$. Throughput gains do not establish a
quality guarantee: at $S=128$, the fraction of draws losing more than
$10^{-3}$ recall ranges from zero to 40/64. The evidence covers one GPU,
$k=10$ and stationary query pools; drift, other $k$ and other devices are
untested.
