\section{Adaptive Hierarchical Refinement}
\label{sec:method2}

\subsection{Criterion}
Original JHQ leaves $\alpha$ externally specified, and the useful budget is
workload dependent: at $\mathit{nprobe}{=}128$ openai3-3072 is flat from
$\alpha{=}4$ over the measured grid while arxiv-768 is still improving at
$\alpha{=}200$, a censored upper endpoint rather than a plateau. A single global
constant therefore either wastes $O(\alpha k d)$ residual work on easy workloads
or filters candidates before accurate refinement on hard ones. We calibrate a
workload-level operating point rather than predict $\alpha$ per query, which
keeps the control path label-free and amortisable.

Let $Q_s$ be $S$ sampled queries from the current workload and $R_{\max}(q)$ the
top-$k$ ID set returned under a generous $\alpha_{\max}$. For a tested $\alpha$
with returned set $R_\alpha(q)$, define the total slot disagreement
\begin{equation}
  D(\alpha) \;=\; \sum_{q \in Q_s}
  \bigl[\, k - \lvert R_\alpha(q) \cap R_{\max}(q) \rvert \,\bigr],
  \label{eq:disagree}
\end{equation}
and select the smallest tested-grid $\alpha$ with $D(\alpha)\le\epsilon$.
Membership asks whether more residual work changes \emph{which} neighbours come
back, ignoring harmless permutations of the same IDs, and $\epsilon$ is a
non-negative integer over the whole sample rather than a per-query fraction ---
so a fixed $\epsilon$ is a stricter test as $S$ grows, which
Section~\ref{sec:rq2} returns to. $S{=}32$ and $\epsilon{=}1$ were our initial
defaults; that section measures what they risk and withdraws the first.
Output stability is observable from the running index where ground-truth recall
is not, but it cannot recover routing losses, certify recall, or see past
$\alpha_{\max}$, so Section~\ref{sec:rq2} validates it against ground truth
offline rather than treating self-agreement as a guarantee.

\subsection{Monotonicity and bisection}
\label{sec:mono}


\begin{proposition}[monotonicity of disagreement]
\label{prop:mono}
Let $\alpha_1 \le \alpha_2 \le \alpha_{\max}$. Under exact top-$c_k$ primary
selection with $c_k$ non-decreasing in $\alpha$, a fixed refined distance, and a
deterministic total tie rule,
$D(\alpha_1) \ge D(\alpha_2) \ge D(\alpha_{\max}) = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact top-$c_k$ selection nests the survivor sets,
$S_{\alpha_1} \subseteq S_{\alpha_2} \subseteq S_{\alpha_{\max}}$. Order
$S_{\alpha_{\max}}$ by the refined distance with the deterministic tie key and
let $R_{\max}$ be its first $k$ elements. Any element of $R_{\max}$ present in
$S_\alpha$ also appears in $\mathrm{top}\text{-}k(S_\alpha)$, since fewer than
$k$ elements of $S_{\alpha_{\max}}$ precede it and hence fewer than $k$ of the
subset $S_\alpha$ do. So $R_\alpha \cap R_{\max} = S_\alpha \cap R_{\max}$,
which can only grow with $\alpha$. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implementation caveat and measured deviation.}
The production scan uses a bounded per-thread selector with
$\texttt{K\_LOCAL}{=}4$, so a thread can discard a globally top-$c_k$ item when
more than \texttt{K\_LOCAL} of them fall in its stride class, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}'s premise is not asserted for the deployed kernel. We measure the gap rather
than hide it behind aggregate recall: with the index pinned, vogue-768 at
$\mathit{nprobe}{=}128$ and $\texttt{BLOCK}{=}512$, raising \texttt{K\_LOCAL}
from 4 to 8 changes 10 of 10{,}000 returned ID positions across 5 of 1{,}000
queries, and \emph{no} query gets a different result set: every difference is
two adjacent ranks swapping inside an identical top-10, so here the bounded
selector reorders ties rather than discarding. Which items tie moves with the
codebook, so this is one index instance, not a constant.
$\texttt{BLOCK}{=}512$ is deliberately the harder collision regime, and
$\texttt{K\_LOCAL}{=}8$ is register-infeasible at the production
$\texttt{BLOCK}{=}1024$.

The consequence for bisection, precisely. Across all 15 calibration traces that probe two or more grid points, $D(\alpha)$ is
non-increasing at the points the search visited. That is evidence, not a proof:
points the search never visited are untested, so we do not claim bisection
returns the global minimum acceptable $\alpha$ on the grid, only that its
stopping behaviour is conservative with respect to the sampled criterion.
Floating-point ties are a separate matter --- the proposition assumes a
deterministic total order, not bitwise-identical association.

Against the prior art of Section~\ref{sec:related}, our contribution here is
narrow: a predictor-free top-$k$ stability signal applied to JHQ's external
residual budget, with its proxy error and economics quantified.

\subsection{Calibration economics}
With $T_{\mathrm{cal}}$ the calibration time and $T_{\mathrm{fixed}}$,
$T_{\mathrm{rule}}$ the per-batch times before and after selection, the
break-even batch count is
$B^{*}=T_{\mathrm{cal}}/(T_{\mathrm{fixed}}-T_{\mathrm{rule}})$ whenever
$T_{\mathrm{rule}}<T_{\mathrm{fixed}}$; Section~\ref{sec:rq2} reports both
terms. Reuse assumes the query distribution, routing configuration, $k$ and
index stay stable --- the price of workload-level rather than per-query
adaptation --- and drift is unmeasured: recalibrating every $H$ batches adds
$T_{\mathrm{cal}}/H$ per batch, but choosing $H$ is a drift-detection problem
outside this evidence. The timer starts after sample copying, so the cost is
algorithm-level.
